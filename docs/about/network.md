# The network

## Origin

NUTRIx grew out of a spring school on machine learning in nutrition, funded by
the Joachim Herz Foundation and organised by Mattea Müller and Jan Voges in 2024.
Many of the network's members first met there and recognised a shared set of
methodological challenges and opportunities for collaboration at the
intersection of nutrition and data science.

## Rationale

Nutrition science is inherently interdisciplinary and is undergoing a
methodological transformation driven by digitalisation and high-dimensional data.
Modern datasets digitised dietary intake records, continuous glucose and
activity monitoring, metabolic and inflammatory biomarkers, and multi-omics
layers (genomics, transcriptomics, proteomics, metabolomics, microbiome) open
the way to a mechanistic, systems-level understanding of how diet influences
health.

## Focus areas

The network concentrates on three interrelated areas:

1. **Machine learning applied to dietary intake and survival data** — prediction
   versus causal inference, estimands, confounding and DAGs, substitution models,
   non-linear associations, and N-of-1 designs.
2. **Transparency and reproducibility in ML-driven nutrition research** — best
   practices, data quality, avoiding data leakage, uncertainty and stability,
   documentation, and FAIR-aligned, shareable workflows.
3. **Integrative analysis of multi-omics datasets in a nutritional context** —
   harmonisation, preprocessing, batch effects, dimensionality reduction,
   multi-omics integration algorithms, and network-based interpretation.

## Objectives

1. **Strengthen interdisciplinary collaboration** at the interface of nutrition
   and data science through regular, structured exchange between early-career
   researchers across institutions.
2. **Address core methodological challenges** in computational nutrition science:
   dietary intake and survival data, multi-omics integration, and reproducibility
   of ML workflows.
3. **Promote open science** by collaboratively building this public, open-source
   knowledge base of reproducible pipelines, tutorials, and methodological
   guidance.
4. **Support early-career development** through peer learning, engagement with
   international experts, and a transatlantic collaboration with the NIH Precision
   Nutrition Training Programme.
5. **Contribute to the wider community** through a symposium and a public online
   workshops and alignment with national and European infrastructures such as ELIXIR.
